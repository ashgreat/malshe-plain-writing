# Other genres

Read the part for the genre that you write. The rules of Part 1 of SKILL.md
still apply, except where a rule below says otherwise.

Each of the first two parts comes from one coauthored article, and another
author led both articles. Treat the structure as his practice. Treat the tone
and the wording as the practice of the author team, and follow the user's
draft where it differs.

## Review and conceptual framework article

The source is a coauthored article in the Journal of the Academy of Marketing
Science (JAMS 2026), on which he is the third of three authors.

1. **Order the article as definition, measurement, neighboring constructs,
   history, evidence, trends, framework by level, and conclusion.**

2. **Open with the practical stakes, and tie them to the construct in the
   first sentence.** The article opens with three epigraphs from company
   founders.
   Example: "As three of the most successful business founders assert, a
   customer-focused strategy is foundational to a firm's enduring success"
   (JAMS 2026).

3. **State the purpose with "this article", and name each audience with what
   it gains.** Scholars, executives, and regulators each get one sentence.
   "We" is rare in the article, at under four uses per 1,000 words.
   Example: "For executives, this article provides a roadmap to measure CSAT,
   predict critical firm outcomes, and develop a customer-focused strategy
   plan." (JAMS 2026)

4. **Define the focal construct in one sentence with "We define X as", and
   then explain the definition point by point.** Each point gets a run-in
   heading that repeats a phrase of the definition.
   Example: "We define customer satisfaction as a post-consumption, evaluative
   judgment that summarizes the extent to which customers perceive they have
   received the value promised by a product or service acquired." (JAMS 2026)

5. **Give each neighboring construct its own run-in subsection named "[focal
   construct] versus [neighbor]".** Open with the published definition of the
   neighbor as a direct quotation with its page. End with a verdict that
   states the relation: antecedent, consequence, or overlap.
   Example: "Thus, the accumulated evidence leads us to conclude that service
   quality is conceptually distinct and empirically correlated with CSAT, and
   is an antecedent of CSAT." (JAMS 2026)

6. **Review the literature by dated period, and write the history in the past
   tense.** The headings give the years and a label. The past tense for
   earlier studies is an exception to rules A9 and D6.
   Example: "Bitner and Hubbert (1994) showed that service quality and CSAT
   are related but distinct constructs, with service quality best
   conceptualized as an antecedent of CSAT." (JAMS 2026)

7. **Add original evidence to the review, and report it with "We" and a verb
   in the past tense.** The article compares scales in a new survey, repeats
   earlier analyses on a new dataset, and counts publications. The detail of
   the method goes to the Web Appendix.
   Example: "We administered a subset of these scales (randomized within
   subject) to a sample of 1,003 consumers recruited from Prolific." (JAMS
   2026)

8. **Open each major section with one sentence that starts with "This
   section", and close it with a subsection headed "Conclusion".** The closing
   subsection states the main points without new citations. The opening
   sentence is an exception to rule B10.
   Example: "This section provides a historical overview with exemplar
   articles (see Web Appendix C for a summary)." (JAMS 2026)

9. **Present the framework one level at a time, with one figure per level.**
   The first sentence of each level has the figure as its subject and names
   the theory behind the model. A named firm or a short case shows the level
   in practice.
   Example: "Next, we briefly describe a real-life case study to illustrate
   the success of CSAT as a strategic framework." (JAMS 2026)

10. **Put the research agenda inside each level, under a run-in heading
    "Future research".** Mark the items with ordinals. Mix direct questions
    with statements that have "Research" or "Future research" as the subject
    and "should" or "can" as the verb. Do not number the questions. A paragraph
    may end on a question. Rule 10 is an exception to rules B4 and C13.
    Examples: "Fourth, how does the utilization of AI and robots affect
    operational inputs and their impact on attribute performance and overall
    satisfaction?" (JAMS 2026) "Sixth, research should more deeply examine
    managerial decision-making in a satisfaction context (Morgan et al.,
    2005)." (JAMS 2026)

11. **Use associational words for the evidence.** The article writes
    "association" or "associated with" 22 times.

12. **End with a short "Conclusion" that states a hope and one claim, with no
    summary of the sections.**
    Example: "We hope that this article inspires new scholars to embrace CSAT
    as a research area." (JAMS 2026)

13. **Keep the tone of the article out of an empirical paper.** The article
    judges the state of the field with words such as "worrisome" and
    "Reassuringly,", and it makes broad claims about scope. Rule A4 applies to
    new text in every genre, so leave such words out unless the user's draft
    has them.

The following formats in the article come from the journal: sentence-case
headings, citations with "&" and a comma, leading zeros, and "Fig. 2" inside a
sentence.

## Methodological commentary or rejoinder

The source is a coauthored rejoinder in Industrial Marketing Management (IMM
2019), on which he is the second of two authors.

14. **Open by naming the target article, its authors, its journal, and its
    goal, and state the goal fairly.** Give the authors a short label, and use
    the label from then on.
    Example: "Zaefarian, Kadile, Henneberg, and Leischnig (2017) (henceforth
    ZKHL) recently published a survey article in this journal titled" (IMM
    2019).

15. **State the disagreement in one short sentence at the start of the second
    paragraph, and then name the kinds of fault.**
    Example: "It is the latter part of the article with which we take issue.
    Several methodologies outlined in ZKHL are either incorrect, missing, or
    misleading." (IMM 2019)

16. **Say what the commentary delivers, and follow with a roadmap
    paragraph.** The roadmap uses "First,", "Then", and "Finally,". The
    roadmap paragraph is an exception to rule B10.

17. **Give credit before each correction.** Say what the other authors did
    well, and say what you will not repeat. Then turn with "However,".
    Example: "Given this backdrop, it is admirable that ZKHL take on the task
    of describing some methods to address endogeneity issues in applied work."
    (IMM 2019)

18. **Quote the disputed passage with its page, and then say plainly that it
    is incorrect and what follows from the error.** Attach the word
    "incorrect" to the description or to the method, and never to the people.
    Example: "However, this will not alleviate the endogeneity problem.
    Indeed, this method will result in biased and inconsistent estimates."
    (IMM 2019)

19. **Build each correction in a fixed order: the principle from the
    textbooks, the intuition in words, the equation, a simulation, and a
    table.** Support the principle with a string of textbook citations.
    Compare the estimators on simulated data whose true values are known.
    Example: "In the following example we demonstrate the correct methodology
    using a simulated dataset and compare with results using the incorrect
    methodology described in ZKHL." (IMM 2019)

20. **Report the comparison as bias against the true value, with the
    numbers.**
    Example: "The bias using the ZKHL estimate is worse than OLS at 1.3416."
    (IMM 2019)

21. **Raise the objection to your own demonstration, and answer it with a
    larger simulation.**
    Example: "It is possible that the empirical results above are a function
    of the single random sample that we generated." (IMM 2019)

22. **Define each technical term where it appears.** The rejoinder puts the
    term in quotation marks and explains it with "in that".
    Example: "Also, the model is overidentified in that there are more
    instruments for the entire model (four) than endogenous variables (two)."
    (IMM 2019)

23. **Offer the materials, and mark what you leave out.** Point to the code,
    the data, and an online appendix. Write "for brevity" when you skip a
    detail, and send the reader to a textbook.
    Example: "The simulated dataset is available from the authors upon
    request." (IMM 2019)

24. **Rank the issues.** Treat the main errors in their own sections, named by
    method. Collect the lesser points in a last section, and announce their
    count.
    Example: "We highlight two additional issues with ZKHL." (IMM 2019)

25. **End by separating opinion from error, and close with a
    recommendation.** Concede the point that is a matter of judgment. Restate
    the error and its consequence. The last sentence tells the reader which
    procedure to follow.
    Example: "We recommend that researchers follow the procedures outlined
    here or in the many econometrics text books cited in this piece rather
    than the procedure suggested in ZKHL." (IMM 2019)

26. **Do not copy the informal wording of the rejoinder.** The rejoinder
    addresses the reader, has four contractions, uses "since" for cause, and
    has a spaced dash for a pause. Rules A2, A10, D10, and E2 apply to new
    text. Keep the informal wording only when the user's draft has it.

## Response to reviewers

No sample is in the corpus, so the rules below apply the rest of the skill to
the genre.

27. **Quote each comment, answer it in order, and state the change with its
    page or section.**

28. **Address the reviewer in the third person.** Write "We thank the
    reviewer for the suggestion." and do not write "you".

29. **Apply the rules of sections A, C, D, and F of SKILL.md to the answers.**
    Apply the full manuscript register to the revised text that you quote.
    Rules B2 and G1 to G9 do not apply to the answers.

30. **Do not claim a change that the manuscript does not contain.** When you
    do not know the page or the wording of the change, write a placeholder.
